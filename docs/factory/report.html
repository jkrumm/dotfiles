1. Verdict

Build a factory that runs the lifecycle of every piece of work and lets Claude Code do the work. The factory (plain code, with cheap models for routine judgment) takes in signals and requests, merges duplicates, heals what a runbook can heal, and queues the rest. It then starts a Claude Code session on the right Max account with the problem and its evidence. That session, the lead, owns the Issue from root cause to a verified production change and reports back; the factory watches the result, closes the Issue and learns. It runs as one new factory service on the mini over Postgres, with Argo as the cockpit on the same box. Warden is switched off, not migrated.

The scarce resources are your attention and the Max windows, not money. In v1's 14-day sample, 32 % of new items reached you, at ≈ 62 notifications a week, and only 3.8 % of those escalations were genuinely yours. IU API spend for all personal gateway work was $183 last week, most of it review.

The target in ten points#

  1. One lifecycle for all work. Incidents, bugs, upkeep findings and your requests are Issues; an initiative is an Issue whose waves are child Issues. Nothing has a side door.
  2. Claude Code owns the work, end to end. A lead finds the root cause, plans, builds (alone or with workers), tests, reviews, ships, checks production and reports. It decides how.
  3. The factory owns the lifecycle, never the reasoning. Intake, dedup by symptom, settle, classify, queue, capacity, watch, learn. Every repo describes itself in project.yaml and one make contract, so the same commands work everywhere.
  4. The brief is the problem, not a diagnosis. Facts, evidence and verified history; no cheap-model root cause or plan for the lead to unlearn.
  5. Cheap models do the routine around it, freely: dedup judging, classification, wording, the changelog, learning write-ups, job summaries, and the review and research tools leads call.
  6. Two Max accounts, one pool, 5-hour aware. Each lead gets a model and effort sized to its Issue and an account with headroom for it. Urgent work takes any headroom, upkeep only what would lapse. Nothing spills to IU API tokens.
  7. A steward watches the whole. One short Claude Code session, daily and on events, looks over all leads, mothers, the queue and both accounts, and steers.
  8. Done means production evidence. The lead ships only with the evidence its change needs, fixes forward when production disagrees. After the hold, the factory runs make verify and the project's journey probes, which no lead writes.
  9. It learns, and turns learning into code. Grouping decisions become labels; verified causes become known fixes; a fix verified twice becomes a runbook or a job check that code runs from then on.
  10. You decide business only: product direction, spend, irreversible data loss, other people. Each decision arrives as a three-line card with a default and an expiry. SLO: under 1 % of items reach you.

3. Principles

Eight rules. A design choice that breaks one is wrong.

  1. The factory runs the lifecycle; Claude Code does the thinking. What a script can decide, a script decides. Routine words and routine judgments (is this the same symptom, which project, how big) go to cheap models. Anything that needs understanding a problem or code goes to a lead.
  2. Max reasons about code, IU API tokens do the routine. Claude Code on the iu and me Max accounts is the only thing that diagnoses or changes a repo, and it runs in herdr, where you can watch it. IU API tokens pay for gateways, OpenCode, Hermes and the tools leads call. A full Max pool means waiting, never spilling.
  3. Hand over the problem, not a conclusion. A brief holds facts, evidence and verified history. A weaker model's guess at the cause is noise a stronger model has to unlearn.
  4. One lifecycle, no side doors. Every piece of work enters as an Issue and leaves with evidence or a stated reason.
  5. Facts are stored, state is derived. The database holds what happened: signals, phases, evidence, decisions, session observations. "Working", "stuck", "waiting" are computed from facts when read, so they cannot drift.
  6. Done means observed in production. A lead saying "done" is a claim; the evidence and the factory's own make verify after the hold are the proof. Wrong in production means fix forward.
  7. A self-improving platform, as code. Agents find the fix; once a fix is verified twice, it becomes a runbook or a job check that code runs from then on. Every rule, runbook, probe, policy threshold and prompt lives in a repo, and changes only through an Issue a lead ships with evidence. That includes the factory's own code: a scorecard measure off target for two weeks becomes an Issue in the factory repo. The factory gets cheaper and better as it learns.
  8. You decide business, nothing else. Product direction, spend, irreversible data loss, other people. Anything else that reaches you is a defect. Everything written for you is short, verdict-first and free of internal names.

Fixed boundaries#

  • Security is the tailnet plus your GitHub user. The factory opens no public endpoint. Sources it cannot reach directly are polled, or arrive through the Slack adapter. On GitHub, only your user's issues and comments start work.
  • No caps on a lead's work. No turn limits, no wall-clock ceilings; the one liveness rule is the idle watchdog, and the steward handles a lead that stops making progress.
  • AGENTS.md only. No CLAUDE.md in any repo.

4. The loop

The factory runs the lifecycle; Claude Code does the work. The factory takes everything in, merges duplicates, heals what a runbook can heal, and queues the rest. When capacity allows, it starts a Claude Code session in herdr, the lead, with the problem and its evidence. The lead owns the Issue: it finds the cause, plans, builds, tests, reviews, ships, checks production and reports back. The factory then watches the result, closes the Issue and learns from it.

Probes are written by nobody who ships. Each deployable project lists a few user-journey probes in project.yaml: can I log in, does the forecast load, does the upload finish. Each passes or fails, never averaged. The factory runs them after every hold, next to make verify. A lead never edits them as part of a fix; changing a probe is an Issue of its own. Tests a model wrote for its own change can give false confidence, and the probes are the check that does not depend on it.

The brief: the problem, never a diagnosis#

A lead reasons better from facts than from a weaker model's guess, so the brief carries no cheap-model root cause and no cheap-model plan.

  • The ask: your words, or what the signals saw: what, where, since when, how often.
  • Evidence: the raw signals, the commands that pull their logs and traces, the project's last deploys and commits.
  • History: closed Issues that looked similar, with the cause and fix their lead verified, marked past, may not apply.
  • The project: its make contract, deploy mode, which paths ship on their own, production targets.
  • Done means: the evidence table above and the factory verbs.
  • Standing orders: decide alone except the four business classes; ask through factory issue ask; no turn or time limits; workers stay on Max.

Classification sets labels (project, kind, urgency, size) and routing (account, model, effort), never a cause; of its output, the brief shows just kind and urgency. The standing orders are identical in every brief, so they are a prompt-cache hit.

Following a running lead#

  • Turn ends are events, not guesses. The lead's Stop hook reports every turn end and its SessionEnd hook the session's end, straight into the Issue's inbox. engrams learned that inferring turn ends from output wedges sessions. Witness reads herdr every minute as the backstop (blocked, walled, dead) and records the account in effect. The lead's factory reports are authoritative, and the server validates each one against its schema.
  • A Stop hook keeps the lead honest. If the lead stops while its Issue is still open and nothing is reported, the hook sends it back with one line: report ship, close, block or ask. Claude Code continues a session when a Stop hook exits with code 2, so nobody types continue.
  • New facts go to the running lead. A signal linked as possibly related to an Issue in work (by the rules, and by the judge once it passes) reaches that lead as a note, not a second session. The lead decides whether it shares the cause.
  • Walls: a walled lead waits for its window and resumes on the same account, cache intact.
  • After ship the lead stays open and idle through the 30-minute hold, so a regression reaches it while its cache is warm. When the hold passes, Witness closes the tab.

The steward#

The orchestrator above the leads and mothers, built for one job: keep the whole factory moving without wasting either subscription.

  • When: 06:30 daily for exceptions only, a full scorecard review on Mondays, and on events:

    • an Issue reopened twice

6. State and durable execution

Postgres on the mini#

One database, factory, on the Postgres 18 that already runs on the mini. Argo moves into the same server as database argo at W5, with its own role and connection limit. Postgres because Argo reads it directly (views, LISTEN/NOTIFY for live updates, no sync job), because JSONB, full-text search and SKIP LOCKED come built in, and because you have hit SQLite locking before.

Durable execution: your one open decision#

My recommendation: no DBOS. Each Issue is a state machine in Postgres, driven by an inbox. The reason is Cortex's engrams, an open-source software factory that runs Claude Code on DBOS on Bun. Its blog argues for durable workflows; its code and ADRs argue the other way for exactly our case (research/2026-10-25-engrams-source.md):

Cortex's blogengrams' code and ADRs
"A software factory is just a durable workflow"ADR 0119: "an entity lifecycle that spans days … must NOT be one long-lived run … The automation is stateful; no run is."
DBOS runs the factorythree workflows, using DBOS only for step checkpoints, send/recv and idempotent workflow ids; cron (croner + a Postgres lease), concurrency claims, retries and recovery are their own code
"every run resumes where it was"DBOS ties recovery to an application version hashed from workflow source, and deploys stranded production runs (253 unread messages, 15 orphaned runs). The fix is a ≈ 2,000-line sweeper (ADR 0104); pinning the version was rejected because it forces patch bookkeeping

An Issue lives for hours or days, so engrams' own rule applies: the Issue is stateful, and no run is. What DBOS would add on top is checkpointing for steps that last seconds, which a deterministic id does as well, and the price is version gating on every deploy. On one Mac mini there is no multi-pod recovery to buy. v2.1's instinct (short runs, state in tables) was right; engrams arrived at the same place the hard way.

8. Dedup and learning

One cause should be one Issue, and a cause seen before should arrive with its verified fix. The deterministic part is proven: grouping F1 is 0.889 against warden's 0.744 on a held-out set. "Seen before" is not proven: v1's known-fix match scored 0.479. Every production platform we looked at gates grouping deterministically and keeps similarity and judgment beside the gate, not in it. None exports corrections as labels, so the learning loop is ours to build (research/2026-10-24-learned-correlation.md).

Two tiers: symptom at intake, cause in the lead#

Your comment named the catch: true dedup needs the root cause, and the root cause needs a smart model. So dedup happens twice:

TierWhoAsksCost
Symptom, at intakefactory: rules; from W4 a cheap judge, if it passes the bake-offis this the same thing happening again?free or one cheap call; never waits
Cause, during investigationthe leaddo these Issues share a cause?part of the lead's work; factory issue merge|split with a reason

Intake only has to catch the obvious repeats: the same monitor flapping, the same error fingerprint in a window. Two Issues that look different but share a cause are the lead's catch. A lead running in a project receives possibly related signals as notes (§4), so a second lead never starts on the same cause. A merge stops the absorbed Issue's lead, if one was running, and hands its notes over.

The judge has to earn its place#

Until W4, the judge is off: ambiguous pairs stay apart and are linked as possibly related. A wrong merge hides work, while a missed merge costs one note to a lead, so the judge must be precise before it may merge anything.

The W4 bake-off, on labelled pairs from the golden set plus every merge and split the leads have made by then:

It reports precision and recall on merges and the cost per pair. The winner must reach a merge precision ≥ 0.95 without dropping grouping F1 below 0.889; if neither does, the judge stays off and leads keep catching duplicates. Recall can grow later; wrong merges cannot be tolerated.

Seen before#

Each new Issue is matched against closed ones by fingerprint and by Postgres full-text search over title, the lead's root cause and fix, ranked by project, subject and recency. The top matches go into the lead's brief as history, marked past, may not apply (§4).

The learning loop#

Every grouping decision (by a rule, the judge, a lead or you) is a row in issue_links with who and why. Every closed Issue carries the lead's verified cause and fix. From those, the learn step (a cheap model) maintains four things:

  1. Labels. Lead merges are ground truth for what intake missed; lead splits are ground truth for what it wrongly joined. A rule whose groups are split more than 10 % of the time over two weeks becomes an upkeep Issue.
  2. Known fixes. Cause and fix per closed Issue, searchable as "seen before".
  3. Runbooks. A fix verified twice for the same cause and subject (a restart, a cache purge, a rerun) becomes an upkeep Issue: "add this as a runbook". A lead adds it to the project's heal: list through review, and from then on code runs it before any lead is spawned.
  4. Calibration. The judge's thresholds are re-checked monthly against the labels and the frozen golden set; a change is accepted only if F1 stays at or above 0.889.

Whether it actually learns is measured, not hoped. You are right that this is the tricky part. The scorecard (§9) tracks, per month:

  • the missed-at-intake rate (lead merges per 100 Issues)
  • the wrong-join rate (lead splits)
  • the seen-before hit rate (a lead confirms the suggested past cause)
  • the runbook share (incidents closed by a runbook, without a lead)

If none of the four improves over two months, the steward opens an Issue that says so.

Not built until measured need: embeddings and vector search. They come when full-text matching misses known fixes you can name, or the split rate says the rules are not enough.

9. Upkeep jobs

A job is a small TypeScript module, not an agent. Code does the looking; a cheap model writes up and groups what it found; a lead does whatever needs understanding. Swamp's ideas, without its runtime: typed steps in code, assertions as first-class steps, nothing spent when everything is fine. Jobs are also where the factory grows more deterministic: a check a lead keeps doing by hand becomes a job assertion.

// factory/jobs/deps.ts
export default job({
  schedule: "0 6 * * 1",
  projects: (p) => p.jobs.includes("deps"),
  async run({ project, probe, summarize, open }) {
    const outdated = await probe.outdated(project);            // bun outdated --json, release ages
    const due = outdated.filter((d) => d.ageDays >= (d.major ? 14 : 3));
    if (due.length === 0) return;                               // green: zero tokens
    const notes = await probe.releaseNotes(due);                // changelogs + migration guides, cached
    const groups = await summarize("group-upgrades", { due, notes }); // one cheap call: grouping, not a plan
    for (const g of groups) await open({ kind: "upkeep", title: g.title, evidence: g }); // a lead plans it
  },
});

A job never edits a repo. Anything that needs a change becomes an Issue and goes to a lead.

FAQ

Can I still just use Claude Code in herdr? Yes. Your sessions are outside the loop unless you hand work to it, and the picker keeps choosing the account.

Both Max accounts are walled and production is down — then what? Settle and runbooks still run; they need no model. Leads wait for the first reset, which on the 5-hour window is never more than five hours away. No IU fallback, by your rule.

Why does review run on IU API tokens if IU must not reason about code? The lead calls it as a tool and weighs the findings; the review decides nothing. A reviewer from another model family sees what the lead's own family misses.

DBOS or not? Your call (§6). My recommendation is no. Cortex's engrams runs a Claude Code factory on DBOS, and its own ADR says a lifecycle that spans days must not be one long-lived run; DBOS's version gating stranded their runs in production. We take engrams' patterns (an inbox per Issue, deterministic ids, claims, replay tests) and leave the engine.

What do I lose during the rewrite? Warden's triage, from W1. Raw alerts keep reaching Slack until each source's adapter lands, Kuma's in W2.

What does it cost? No new subscription. Cheap models are used freely within your €500; review is the line to watch, and depth gating keeps it in bounds. The rewrite costs ≈ 20–40 % of one iu week.

How do I steer something in flight? Type into the lead's tab in herdr, or use Argo intents (steer, pause, stop), a comment on its plan (in plannotator until W5, in Artifacts after), Hermes, or factory issue from any shell.

What will I notice first? After W1, a one-line request becomes a shipped change with a production screenshot and a three-line summary, without you touching it. After W2, a container that stops comes back on its own, and you read one line about it in the morning instead of an alert in Slack.

Is the steward the "daddy orchestrator"? Yes. It is short runs rather than an always-on session, so its context never rots and it costs minutes a day. During the rewrite, the build orchestrator plays the same part (§5, §12).
